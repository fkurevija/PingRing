## 📡 Mesh & Connectivity
- **Live-key-gated BLE presence**: signed V5 advertisements update proximity only after a fresh registered-key nonce response over the member's Nearby link. Proof lasts ten seconds and renews on demand; captured advertisements cannot renew it. This proves key control, not physical distance, and does not replace UWB ranging.
- **One-tap hosting**, no server registration. UWB can be enabled or disabled; Nearby Connections selects its transport, with no app-level Wi-Fi Direct selector. **Every switch in PingRing Settings applies the moment it is touched** — there is no Save and no Cancel, matching App Settings, and leaving is the back arrow. The staged Save/Cancel model was a trap rather than a safety net: a Cancel button sitting next to a Save button makes the back arrow look like a third way to discard your work, so a member who changed a setting and navigated away had no way to tell whether it had been kept. The controls here are all trivially reversible by flipping them back, so a confirm step bought nothing and cost a way to lose a change by accident. Each change is persisted and broadcast to the other members immediately, and the screen does **not** navigate away afterwards — it used to pop the back stack on Save, which with immediate application would have thrown the member back to the radar after the first of two switches.
- **Connection recovery prompt** for guests after the authenticated host link is unavailable for the configured interval (enabled by default at 2 minutes; configurable from 1–10 minutes in App Settings). Choose **Leave PingRing**, snooze for 2 minutes, or silence prompts for the current Ring. The prompt appears only while the radar is foregrounded and does not claim whether the host is out of range or ended the Ring while disconnected.
- **Adjustable join timeout** (App Settings, 10–120 seconds, default 60). How long joining waits for the host before reporting failure. Set it short for a host who is watching the screen and will answer in seconds; set it long for one who is busy. A wait below the host's 45-second approval window means giving up while they are still deciding, so a short setting can fail a join that was about to be allowed — the default of 60 s does not have that problem.
- **Admission control**: the Host can close a PingRing to arrivals mid-event via **Prevent New Users From Joining**; authenticated Nearby requests are then refused and the Radar's QR button hides until it is re-enabled. Joining is by the host's QR code or their six-character code, and nothing else — a PingRing is never listed on a phone that was not told about it. A guest who scanned the host's own QR code is admitted directly, because showing that code is the host offering the ring; a guest holding only a typed code is put to the host to allow or deny, and unanswered requests expire after 45 seconds. Nearby's shared digits must be compared on both phones before the connection is accepted.
  - This is deliberately the host's *only* standing control. There was previously a second switch that held every join, including QR ones, for a per-join decision. It made the host a queue-keeper for guests it had personally invited, and a guest could not see why their own radar was behaving as it did.
- **Bounded capacity**: max **8 participants** (1 Host + 7 Guests), enforced by the Host (`8/8 - Full`). This bounds the amount of connection, message and ranging work; it is not a measured performance guarantee.
- **Bounded peer relay mesh**: admitted members connect over Nearby `P2P_CLUSTER`; the host remains the only admission authority, and new members must reach it directly. Signed-origin messages use duplicate suppression, a seven-hop limit, and a bounded memory-only queue that replays when links return. Signed five-second presence heartbeats can show a remote member without inventing RSSI, distance, or direction.
- **Confirmed graceful departures**: when a guest leaves while connected, an authenticated notice reaches the host, which relays it to the group. A lost link or crashed app cannot send that notice; silence still means *unreachable*, not confirmed gone.
- **Current ranging sources**: fresh UWB provides distance and bearing; compatible Wi-Fi Aware phones can supply focused phone-to-phone RTT distance without venue infrastructure. GPS coordinates are **always** shared while a PingRing runs — rounded to approximately 10 m and published every ten seconds — and shown as a coordinate-derived distance with uncertainty. This is not a switch: it was host-controlled before, which meant a guest could not tell whether the feature was live, so two phones could silently disagree and a ring could be missing half its sources. Neither RTT nor GPS draws a directional needle; GPS never triggers close-range haptics. Signed BLE RSSI remains the local proximity fallback on capable controllers. Radio delivery and accuracy remain [unverified on hardware](roadmap.md#unverified).
- **Automatic fallback** from sub-metre UWB to signed BLE RSSI proximity when UWB is unsupported or unavailable and the Bluetooth controller supports extended advertising. The RSSI path is smoothed over a rolling **2-second window** — the mean is taken in dBm and the distance curve applied once to the result, so one reflected or dropped advert cannot move the readout. A time window rather than a per-sample average, because the advertising rate varies by peer and the app only ever sees the reports that arrive. On unsupported hardware, RSSI proximity fails closed rather than accepting V4 group-authenticated packets.

## 👤 Identity & Join
- **Custom usernames**, saved locally via DataStore and pre-filled on future sessions. Names are enforced unique within a session, with an alert on collision.
- **Join via QR** (ML Kit, on the Host's on-screen code) or a **6-character PingRing Code** (`e.g. AB12CD`). Code fields force ALL CAPS and accept at most six characters — a pasted code cannot leave extra digits in the field to be refused at the moment of joining — and usernames use Word Capitalization. The Radar's QR button re-opens the code mid-event for latecomers. The host setup panel and that dialog share one `PingRingCodeBlock` — QR, the code in a labelled monospace chip, and Max Capacity — so the invitation a guest is given does not change shape between the two; only the QR is drawn larger in the dialog. Capacity is in both because it is part of what is being read out, and the radar states it nowhere else. Long-pressing the code copies it, with a **Copy PingRing Code** accessibility action so the clipboard is reachable without the gesture.
- **What may be typed**: `TextInputPolicy` is the single rule every free-text field goes through, and it is deliberately additive about what survives. Emoji, combining marks, Devanagari conjuncts, right-to-left scripts and zero-width joiners all pass through untouched, because a filter that strips "special characters" breaks every name outside ASCII. What is dropped is inert formatting — zero-width spaces, the BOM used as filler, soft hyphen and the bidi embedding/override controls — since those are how two different strings are made to render identically and so defeat the name uniqueness check. Control characters become spaces rather than vanishing, and length is counted in **code points**, not `Char`s, so a cap cannot slice a surrogate pair in half. ZWJ and ZWNJ are kept on purpose: ZWJ builds family and profession emoji and Indic conjuncts, and ZWNJ changes the meaning of Persian text.
- **The relay host is validated, not cleaned**: it is the one field that becomes a socket address, so an address that is not `host` or `host:port` is refused outright and the previous value is kept, rather than being repaired into something the user did not type.
- **Invite secret stays in the QR**: each event gets a random 256-bit secret. The six-character code is only a public locator; code-only joins require host approval. BLE never carries the secret.
- **Join verification**: both devices compare Nearby's shared digits before accepting. Code-only joins wait for host approval; unanswered requests expire after two minutes and are cleaned up so the guest can retry.

## 🚨 Beacons & Chat
- **Intent presets**: ✨ Found Something, 📍 Regroup Here, ⚠️ Caution, 🚨 Emergency, ⚡ Custom — with 5 colour palettes (Crimson, Amber, Emerald, Cyan, Purple) and 5 vibration rhythms (SOS/Urgent, Rapid Alert, Steady Pulse, Gentle Pulse, No Vibration). **Alert repetition is a per-device preference, not part of the beacon payload**: whoever raises a beacon chooses its rhythm, but whether the alert plays once or repeats — and how long the pause between repeats is — is the receiving member's own choice, because a sender must not be able to dictate how often seven other phones buzz. The gap is carried inside the vibration waveform rather than scheduled around it, so it cannot drift or keep running after the alert is cancelled. A repeating alert is cancelled when the beacon ends, when it is stopped, or when the member switches back to "Once"; a beacon whose rhythm is "No Vibration" stays silent whatever the repetition setting says. Beacons illuminate radar rings, peer pins and wave animations on receivers. **Each beacon can be silenced individually** from its entry in the transcript: the bell button stops this device buzzing for that beacon and nothing else. Silencing is keyed on the beacon id rather than the sender, because "this one is annoying" is not "that person is annoying" — the next beacon the same member raises is a different alarm. It is a purely local decision, never transmitted. **Silencing suppresses the alert only and never the beacon itself**: a silenced beacon is still rendered in the transcript, still tints the radar, still counts as active and still ends when its sender stops it, because a member must not be able to mute themselves out of seeing a real emergency.
- **The raiser hears it too**: the device that activates a beacon plays its own vibration pattern at that moment. It used to vibrate only on receiving phones, which read as a broken feature on the one device the user was holding.
- **Offline group chat** with sender-labelled bubbles and **12 fixed bubble colours** — red, orange, amber, yellow, lime, green, teal, cyan, blue, indigo, violet and pink, plain named hues rather than dark tinted shades, with bubble text switching between black and white per colour so every one stays readable. A colour is drawn at random when a member enters a PingRing and is changeable at any time from **Chat Settings** — a button between the beacon controls and the transcript. **A bubble's colour identifies a person, so it follows the person rather than the moment the message was written**: changing your colour repaints every message you have already sent, and when another member changes theirs their earlier bubbles change too, so a transcript is never split down the middle by a colour change. There is no custom picker and no theme-colour option: two phones drawing the same member's messages differently is exactly what makes a crowded transcript unreadable, so the palette is fixed and shared. **Each colour belongs to one member at a time**: the host arbitrates, a colour somebody already holds is dimmed and cannot be picked, and switching hands the old one back so it becomes available to everyone else. The chosen colour travels with new messages. Messages from older builds carry no colour and render in the plain theme style — until that same member sends a coloured message, at which point their older bubbles take it too. The chat window floats over the radar while typing, then returns to its docked position.
- **Beacons live in the chat transcript**: a beacon that is *running* is pinned to the newest end, where a current thing belongs and the eye already is. When it ends it moves back to where it was raised, ordered by timestamp among the messages around it. Nothing about how a beacon looks changes either way — same entry, same styling, just a different place in the conversation. A peer that disconnects mid-beacon has it ended rather than dropped, since it happened.
- **Host-preserved history**: the Host keeps the transcript for the life of the PingRing and replays it to peers who join or reconnect.
- **Configurable notifications**: toggle chat push, sound and vibration device-wide in **App Settings → Notifications**, and chat push per-PingRing in **PingRing Settings**. Notifications are suppressed while you are already watching the radar with the screen on and unlocked; the device can still vibrate or sound.

## 🧭 Radar, Theming & Hardware Adaptation
- **Sensor-fusion tracking**: rotation-vector, magnetometer and accelerometer orient peer vectors into world space while a non-blocking UWB session supplies sub-metre distance, azimuth and elevation for the focused member.
- **UWB cross-checked against BLE, and marked when obstructed.** UWB's dominant error in a crowd is not noise but **obstruction**: a body between two phones blocks the direct first path, the round trip is measured off a reflection, and the distance reads *too long* — often by metres, always in the same direction. BLE signal strength degrades under that same body, and it is an independent measurement of the same geometry, which is what makes it a usable check. So the RSSI estimate is now kept alive while ranging (it used to be discarded the moment a UWB fix arrived, making the two mutually exclusive) and the radar reports **Cross-checked**, **Someone may be in the way**, or **Distance estimate only** when there is only one source. This costs nothing extra — the BLE adverts are already arriving for discovery — and it matters most at exactly the kind of event the app is for.
- **Proximity haptics** under 2 m; **stale peers normally expire** from the radar after 15 s, while the focused peer may remain during its retained UWB window. The RSSI distance is the mean of a rolling 2-second window in dBm, with one window per peer, so a single reflected or dropped advert cannot move the readout and eight members cannot average into each other. It is a window in *time* rather than a count of samples on purpose: a sample-weighted average would smooth differently depending on how often a peer happens to advertise, which the app cannot see.
- **Weak BLE signal warning** for the tracked member, or a group summary when other members are fading. It requires a smoothed signal at or below -85 dBm for 1.5 s and clears above -80 dBm to avoid flicker. It warns that BLE proximity may disappear; it is not a measurement of Nearby transport health.
- **Who is tracked, and who decides**: a **Tracking** dropdown names whoever the radar and the single UWB session are following, and changes it — the per-row buttons can also set it, and tapping the tracked row again returns to the automatic choice. With nothing chosen, a guest follows the **host** and the host follows **whoever joined first** — role-based rather than nearest, because "nearest" changes underneath the user as people walk. Every member advertises its arrival time, so "first" means first rather than first-heard: a member who wanders in late and stands nearest is not mistaken for the one who has been there since the start.
  - The picker is **always available to every member**. There was a **Track Host** setting that locked every guest to the host and disabled their picker, visibly but with no way to act on it — the host ended up controlling what every guest's own screen pointed at, and a guest tracking another guest was something the host could prevent. Choosing who to follow is now always the local user's decision. Everyone still *receives* every message regardless of who is tracked.
- **Directional radar**: a needle points at the tracked member and a lit arc on the ring widens as they approach — a sliver when far, the whole ring within arm's reach (1.5 m). Width is spaced logarithmically, so each doubling of distance gets an equal share of the arc. With no UWB bearing the ring stays plain - signal strength only tints it - rather than inventing a direction.
- **Opt-in screen-reader radar summary**: when enabled in **App Settings → Accessibility**, the radar Canvas exposes the tracked member, distance or signal state, direction when known, and any active beacon as one spoken description, including GPS uncertainty.
- **Accessible feedback controls**: Reduce Motion stops continuous Home/beacon pulses and honors disabled system animations. Proximity Haptics remains on by default and is independent of beacon/notification vibration. Optional Spoken Radar Updates announce stable state changes through a polite screen-reader live region at most once every ten seconds, only on the foreground radar without an open dialog.
- **UWB sessions survive a transport drop**: if a member walks out of transport range while still being tracked, the ranging session stays open for up to 45 s, shortened to 20 s when radio load is high. Ending or leaving the ring discards every retained address.
- **Last known UWB direction**: after 2 s without a fresh UWB measurement, the radar freezes the last world-space bearing and labels it with its age for up to two minutes. It does not infer a direction from BLE RSSI; a BLE-only peer has no directional last-known marker. A fresh BLE sighting or UWB fix clears the marker.
- **Theming**: a fixed violet brand palette applied throughout, honouring the system's light/dark setting, an adaptive violet-and-white radar icon, and a monochrome `ic_launcher_monochrome.xml` for Android 13+ launchers. **Material You dynamic colour is deliberately not used** — see [Known limitations](roadmap.md#known-limitations).
- **Localized interface** in English, Croatian, Spanish, French, Canadian French, German, and Russian. Android 13 and newer can select PingRing's language independently in the system app-language settings; earlier Android versions follow the device language.
- **Device-aware permissions**: unsupported hardware (`FEATURE_BLUETOOTH`, `FEATURE_WIFI`, `FEATURE_LOCATION`, camera, UWB, vibrator) skips its permission prompt, and its UI is disabled with an explanatory label instead of an ungrantable dialog.
