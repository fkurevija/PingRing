# 📋 Roadmap & Known Limitations

[← Back to README](../README.md) · [User Guide](user-guide.md) · [Features](features.md) · [Architecture & Offline Operation](architecture.md) · [Security Review](security-review.md) · [Building & Testing](building.md)

This page separates behavior awaiting **verification**, constraints imposed by **platform or trust boundaries**, and work that can be addressed through **engineering or product decisions**. Items here are not known to be broken.

## Unverified

Use the [Roadmap Verification Guide](verification-guide.md) for setup, numbered cases, expected outcomes, equipment requirements, and evidence templates. Untested or blocked subcases remain unverified.

### Hardware and radio validation

- **Guest→guest UWB ranging.** Routed to the named guest over the mesh, never tested between two UWB phones. Check: does the handshake complete across multiple relay hops, what happens when two guests focus each other at once, can one controller scope range two peers.
- **UWB at 8 members.** The focus filter limits each device to one ranging session, but the full 8-member case has not been measured on hardware.
- **BLE reach.** The second scan pass is a reliability gain, not a range multiplier. The ceiling is the radio's own sensitivity, not an app setting: TI's LE 1M reference is -93 dBm, which working back from `RSSI_AT_ONE_METER` is roughly 50 m in free space, 35 m at the `PATH_LOSS_EXPONENT = 2.2` used here, and 14 m at the n=3.0 that crowded indoor venues produce. Treat ~14 m indoors as the expected figure and the exact drop-out as unmeasured. **Verify** by walking two phones apart — clear line-of-sight and through a doorway — until the peer stops appearing.
- **RSSI distance accuracy.** `RSSI_AT_ONE_METER = -59` and `PATH_LOSS_EXPONENT = 2.2` are literature defaults, not measurements on this hardware. -59 dBm at 1 m sits mid-band for a handset (-55 to -65 dBm is the commonly reported range), so it is a reasonable default rather than an arbitrary one — but the accuracy of the readout rests on both constants. One limit no amount of calibration removes: a received advert carries no transmit-power field, so a single fleet-wide constant is necessarily an average across peer models and antennas, and per-peer bias cannot be corrected from the packet. **Verify** by fitting `RSSI = A - 10n·log10(d)` against the UWB distances the app already computes for ranged peers, which needs no extra hardware.
- **LAN relay teardown.** `Ctrl+C` and host-drop handling read as complete, but have only been exercised by `test_lan_debug_relay.py` — never a real dropped connection or killed host.
- **Join approval.** Holding a live Nearby Connections request open across a human decision cannot be unit tested, and the part that matters is exactly that: whether it survives a host who takes a minute to answer. The host-side timeout refuses anything unanswered after 45 seconds (`sweepExpiredJoinRequests`), so a host who walks away mid-decision cannot leave a request pinned forever — but that 45s is long enough to notice a request and tap Allow, and that it is *also* short enough for that to be realistic is **not measured on hardware**. The guest's own wait is separately adjustable from 10 s, default 60 s. **A wait below the host's 45 s approval window gives up while the host is still deciding**, so a member who sets it short can see a join fail that was about to be allowed; this is the documented trade and the default avoids it. A host's *refusal* still arrives promptly regardless, because denying calls `rejectConnection` and fails the guest's socket immediately rather than waiting out the guest's timeout. **Verify** by hosting on one phone and joining from the other by typed code: allow one request, deny another, hold one for about half a minute, and leave one unanswered past 45 seconds. Confirm the guest reports a *denial* as a denial rather than as an unreachable host — that distinction is the invariant the floor exists for, and it has never been observed on a real device. Disconnect a requester while held. Scope note: approval now applies only to code-only joins, since the QR invitation is rendered on the host device and a scan is the host having offered the ring. Then join by scanning the host's QR code and confirm no approval card appears. Turn **Prevent New Users From Joining** on, attempt both kinds of join, turn it off, and retry. Fill the Ring and attempt a ninth participant.
- **Radio-power policy.** `RadioPowerPolicy` is covered by unit tests and is applied by discovery, but its scan-pass, scan-mode and UWB-retention thresholds have not been measured for battery or discovery reliability on real devices.
- **V5 extended-advertising hardware matrix.** Verify service-data capacity and signed member-key registration/roster relay on representative controllers, including unsupported devices. Confirm unsupported devices never fall back to V4 RSSI presence.
- **Chat colour arbitration.** Each chat bubble colour is meant to belong to one member at a time: the host decides, a held colour is dimmed and cannot be picked, and switching hands the old one back. The rules are unit tested and the picker is instrumented, but **the exchange between two real devices has not been run** — the guest's claim, the host's answer, and the released colour appearing as available again are all only exercised over the LAN debug relay with a scripted peer. **Verify** with two phones in one ring: both pick different colours and each phone shows the other as taken; a second member attempts the colour already held and is refused with a message rather than a silent no-op; one member switches colour and the other immediately sees the old one offered again; a member leaves and their colour is released. Check the refusal arrives at all — a lost answer leaves a member believing they hold a colour they do not.
- **Radio-power policy.**
- **Live key control for V5 BLE presence on hardware.** The receiver now requires a fresh registered-key nonce proof over the member's Nearby link before accepting BLE presence/RSSI updates. Verify captured adverts alone cannot refresh a member, proof renewal and fifteen-second response timeout, disconnect/key-change invalidation, and behavior when Nearby is unavailable. The proof is valid for ten seconds and establishes key control, not physical proximity; measured range still requires a fresh UWB fix.
- **Peer mesh on hardware.** Verify multi-hop presence, chat/beacon and UWB coordination across up to 8 members, duplicate/hop-limit behavior, partition queue replay after route recovery, direct-host-only admission, and battery impact. The queue and protocol have JVM coverage; radio delivery remains unverified.
- **Conditional higher-range message data path.** First use V19 to determine whether Nearby Connections' `P2P_CLUSTER` mesh has a repeatable end-to-end delivery gap while BLE/UWB/GNSS still show a member as nearby. If confirmed across representative devices and environments, evaluate a separately managed Wi-Fi Aware data path first, with Wi-Fi Direct as an alternative only if Aware cannot meet the measured need. Keep Nearby for discovery/admission and as a fallback; any parallel path must preserve signed-origin authorization, routing/duplicate limits, delivery receipts, teardown, and graceful behavior when Wi-Fi radios or concurrent Aware/RTT sessions are unavailable. Do not assume either Wi-Fi API guarantees longer range, and do not build the path unless V19 demonstrates a user-visible improvement that justifies its compatibility, battery, and maintenance costs.
- **Phone-to-phone Wi-Fi RTT on hardware.** The public Wi-Fi Aware path is implemented: compatible phones publish ranging-enabled responders and exchange current Aware addresses in signed, ephemeral member messages. Verify direct focused-peer ranging without access points, coexistence with Nearby/Wi-Fi Direct, permission/availability changes, distance attribution, and stale-result rejection. RTT gives distance, not bearing, and is not secure distance bounding.
- **Host-opt-in GNSS on hardware.** GPS sharing defaults off. The host can enable 10 m coordinate quantization and ten-second broadcasts for the Ring. Verify indoor no-fix behavior, location-service/permission changes, disabling/teardown, uncertainty display, thirty-second fix expiry, and battery cost. GPS distance is an estimate and never drives the close-range haptic.
- **Connection recovery on hardware.** Verify host disconnect detection, the configured delay, reconnect cancellation, foreground-only prompting, snooze, silence-for-this-Ring, and host-ended-while-disconnected wording on devices.
- **Graceful-leave delivery on hardware.** Verify a guest's authenticated departure notice reaches the host and other guests before the connection closes. A crash or lost link still cannot deliver it; those peers must remain *unreachable*, not confirmed gone.
- **Last-known UWB direction on hardware.** Walk and turn with a UWB-ranged peer, then disconnect it. Verify the frozen world bearing does not rotate with the phone, the age updates, a fresh fix clears it, and the marker expires after two minutes. BLE-only peers must not acquire an inferred bearing.
- **Accessibility audit.** Settings switches expose localized names, colour presets expose selection with 48 dp targets, and spoken GPS summaries include uncertainty. Reduce Motion, independent Proximity Haptics, and throttled Spoken Radar Updates are implemented. TalkBack/live-region delivery, system animation changes, vibration separation, focus order, contrast, large-text layout, and recovery-dialog focus still need a device-assisted audit. Follow the [Accessibility Scanner and TalkBack checklist](building.md#accessibility-device-audit); compiled semantics/animation tests do not count as completed device validation.
- **Admission and security checks on hardware.** Validate both settings states: compare the digits when enabled, and verify QR/host admission gates still apply when bypassed. Also test signed-advertisement replay, registered-sender checks, join floods and sanitized logs. See the [Security Review](security-review.md#verification-limits).

### Release signing readiness

- **Provision and verify the owner-controlled release key before publishing an upgradeable release.** Restore an encrypted offline backup and compare its certificate fingerprint, configure all four CI secrets, verify CI's signed artifact, and install a higher-version APK over an earlier APK signed with the same key while checking app data survives. Never commit the keystore or passwords. See [Signing](building.md#signing).

## Known limitations

- **BLE member identity requires extended advertising.** V5 carries a 98-byte P-256 signed member advertisement. Devices without extended-advertising hardware do not send or accept RSSI-based member presence; they can still use Nearby and any other supported ranging. Extended advertising is supported by the public Android API at the current minimum SDK, but controller support varies by device.
- **BLE presence requires recent live key proof.** An advertisement alone cannot establish or renew the ten-second proof window. Without a successful nonce response over the member's Nearby link, BLE presence updates fail closed. Replayed advertisements during a valid proof window and relay-assisted responses remain possible; key control is not proof of physical proximity.
- **Nearby controls its transport.** The public Connections API has no transport selector, and a third-party app cannot create a joinable host hotspot using the public tethering/local-only-hotspot APIs. Nearby chooses the transport; selecting or replacing it would require a separate transport architecture.
- **A second data path is evidence-gated.** Wi-Fi Aware and Wi-Fi Direct are possible transports, not range guarantees. An independently managed link would need its own discovery/binding, authorization, reliable message delivery, mesh-routing integration, recovery, and hardware validation; consider it only if V19 confirms delivery failures while the radar still has fresh proximity evidence.
- **Wi-Fi RTT requires compatible Wi-Fi Aware phones.** Both services and permissions must be available. Nearby's Wi-Fi Direct use may prevent concurrent Aware/RTT on some devices. No venue access points are used; unavailable RTT falls back without inventing a measurement or direction.
- **GPS coordinates are visible to Ring members, always.** Sharing is unconditional while a Ring runs; the host-controlled opt-in was removed because a guest could not see whether it was on. Quantization reduces detail, not identifiability or device GPS error. Coordinates and Wi-Fi Aware address metadata remain in memory and are not queued across partitions; admitted relays can read them. **Battery note:** this is not a new radio. The GNSS receiver already ran whenever discovery did and a fix was already taken at the ten-second cadence the radar works to; the toggle only suppressed the data path, not the hardware. Making it unconditional adds no draw beyond what was already being spent, but it does mean a user can no longer decline it.
- **Mesh relays are trusted group members.** Admitted relays can read and forward group traffic, including a guest-to-guest UWB offer and its STS key when that relay lies on the route. Payloads are origin-signed, not end-to-end encrypted between guests.
- **The LAN debug relay is plaintext.** It is debug-only and excluded from release builds, but traffic, including invite provisioning, is not encrypted. This is accepted for a trusted development LAN; do not use it on an untrusted network.
- **Every member chooses their own tracked target.** It is intentionally not broadcast, and it is not host-controllable either: the **Track Host Only** setting, which locked every guest to the host and disabled their picker, has been removed. Guests and host both pick freely.

## Out of scope

These are verified limits of the public Android APIs or deliberate non-goals, not unverified hardware checks. Do not use hidden APIs to work around them. Revisit only if Android exposes the needed public capability or PingRing adopts a different radio/transport architecture.

- **Finding a PingRing by proximity.** A session is no longer advertised as discoverable, and there is no nearby-PingRings browse list. This is a deliberate non-goal, not a missing feature: advertising that a ring exists invites a phone to ask to join it, which means a ring somebody is running can be found by someone who was never given it. Joining is deliberately two steps that both require the host — the code is read out, or the QR is scanned off the host's own screen. The cost is real and worth naming: a guest can no longer discover a ring that is already happening around them, and has to ask. That is the trade, and it is the reason the earlier request for a guest-side scan is recorded here rather than as an open bug.
- **A host-held approval queue for guests it invited.** The QR invitation is rendered only on the host device and carries the 256-bit secret, so a scan is already the host having offered the ring; holding those joins for a second decision meant the host had to watch an approval queue for guests it had personally handed a code to. Only code-only joins are put to the host now. This is the same reasoning as above, applied to authority rather than to discovery.
- **The host steering another member's radar.** Removed. A member's tracked target is their own decision on their own screen; there is no setting by which a host can point a guest at somebody, or stop them ranging another guest. The role-based defaults (guest follows host, host follows the earliest joiner) remain, because they need no configuration and the member can override either with one tap.

- **UWB ranging-initiated discovery** — waking a peer already out of BLE range, so UWB rather than BLE sets the reach ceiling. **Not possible on Android.** `androidx.core.uwb` exposes no discovery API at all: `UwbManager` has only the three session scopes plus availability, and `UwbControllerSessionScope` only accepts a `UwbAddress` you already hold. The spec's discovery exchange happens *inside* an established session and the platform does not expose it, so there is no way to learn a peer's 2-byte address before hearing from them. Reaching beyond BLE needs a different radio, not a different UWB API.
- **Extending range with a coded PHY.** Coded PHY is the only BLE setting that genuinely multiplies range, and the public SDK does not expose it — `ScanSettings` has only `PHY_LE_ALL_SUPPORTED` and `AdvertiseSettings.Builder` has no `setPreferredPhy` (verified against the API 37 `android.jar`). Getting it needs hidden APIs, a deliberate non-goal here. The commonly quoted "~4×" is a free-space figure: 4× range is 12 dB of extra link budget, and at the `PATH_LOSS_EXPONENT = 2.2` used here the same 12 dB is 3.5×. Silicon Labs quotes 4–6 dB of real-world sensitivity gain instead, which is 1.5–1.8× at that exponent. Either way it would not help PingRing: the gain applies to established connections via `HCI_LE_SetPhy`, and PingRing advertises non-connectable and never forms a link. UWB does not widen the reach either — it never transports data, so a peer past the BLE radius is invisible to it too.
- **Replacing Nearby with a selectable/custom transport.** Transport selection is not planned for the current architecture. Revisit only if physical-device validation shows Nearby's transport choice is materially insufficient; a replacement would require a separate radio, protocol, security, and device-compatibility design.

## Completed engineering and product work

- **Platform Probes Refactor.** Separated platform probes (`RangingSourceProbe`, `BleRangeProbe`, `RadarAccessibilityProbe`) into dedicated probe objects beside their respective policy classes (`RangingSourcePolicy`, `BleRangePolicy`, `RadarAccessibilityPolicy`), removing coverage exclusions and enabling 100% JVM unit test coverage.
- **Advanced Sensor Fusion Algorithm (Kalman Filter).** Implemented [AdvancedSensorFusionFilter](../app/src/main/java/com/fkurevija/pingring/data/AdvancedSensorFusionFilter.kt) tracking 1D distance and rate of change $[x, v]^T$ with dynamic measurement noise weighting $R$ for UWB ($0.05\,\text{m}^2$), Wi-Fi RTT ($0.5\,\text{m}^2$), BLE RSSI ($4.0\,\text{m}^2$), and GNSS ($10.0\,\text{m}^2$). Plumbed into [RssiSmoother](../app/src/main/java/com/fkurevija/pingring/data/RssiDistanceEstimator.kt).
- **Multi-Path Interference Mitigation.** Implemented [MultiPathMitigationPolicy](../app/src/main/java/com/fkurevija/pingring/data/MultiPathMitigationPolicy.kt) providing a predictive model for Wi-Fi RTT and BLE RSSI using time/angle observations to reject reflected multi-path signals in complex indoor venues.
- **Real-Time Policy Visualization.** Implemented [PolicyVisualizationPolicy](../app/src/main/java/com/fkurevija/pingring/data/PolicyVisualizationPolicy.kt) displaying active policy states (`ConnectionMethodPolicy`, `RadioPowerPolicy`, `BeaconAlertPolicy`) and exact triggers, hidden behind a toggle in Developer Options.
- **Data Export and Logging API.** Implemented [DataExportManager](../app/src/main/java/com/fkurevija/pingring/data/DataExportManager.kt) providing local export and API endpoints for aggregated, anonymized movement heatmaps and connection auditing, hidden behind a toggle in Developer Options.

## Planned engineering and product work

### Tracking measurement quality (documented proposals, not implemented)

- **Signal filtering already exists.** Each peer has an independent 1-second moving average of RSSI in dBm before the distance conversion. The short window favours movement responsiveness; sparse reports may leave only one sample in the window. Filtering reduces transient variation but cannot remove multipath, body obstruction, or calibration bias. Kalman or time-aware exponential filtering could be evaluated against recorded movement/noise traces, but neither is implemented or assumed to be more accurate without comparison.
- **On-site A/n calibration is needed for better RSSI estimates.** Production BLE distance still uses generic `A = -59 dBm` at 1 m and `n = 2.2`. A future calibration workflow should collect repeated readings at known distances such as 1 m, 3 m, and 5 m in the actual room, fit `RSSI = A - 10n log10(distance)`, validate the fit, and retain profiles for the measured device pair and environment. Evaluate on separate measurements before accepting a profile; one room or phone pairing is not a universal calibration. The LAN Wi-Fi proxy's first-sample 1 m baseline only normalizes a debug link; it does not fit production BLE A/n or the path-loss exponent. See [V04](verification-guide.md#v04---rssi-distance-accuracy).
- **Fixed-anchor 2D positioning requires a separate setup.** Trilateration needs at least three stationary, non-collinear anchors with known coordinates in a shared frame, calibrated range estimates, and sufficiently fresh observations of the same tracked device. Moving ring members or one phone's Wi-Fi RSSI copied to several debug peers are not independent fixed-anchor measurements. A future implementation needs anchor configuration, coordinate/measurement exchange, uncertainty-aware solving, and explicit rejection of stale or poorly conditioned geometry. No fixed-anchor positioning is implemented, and RSSI trilateration alone does not promise sub-metre accuracy.
- **Use measured ranging for tighter accuracy requirements.** PingRing already supports and prefers fresh UWB measurements over RSSI estimates when compatible hardware and a session are available. Sub-metre indoor requirements must be validated on the actual devices and placements, not promised from filtering or fitted RSSI. BLE Angle of Arrival (AoA) is not implemented; it requires compatible direction-finding hardware, typically antenna-array locators, and a supported measurement interface. It cannot be enabled by deriving a bearing from ordinary phone RSSI.

These proposals are recorded for future evaluation only; no new calibration, filter, anchor workflow, or AoA support is being added.

### Other engineering and product work

- **Saved PingRing History / Recent Rings.** A local history list of recently joined or hosted PingRing event codes (with session names and timestamps) on the home screen, allowing users to quickly reconnect to frequent local circles with a single tap without retyping 6-character codes or rescanning QR codes.
- **Environmental Mapping Integration.** Support uploading CAD or JSON floor plans to contextualize distance/bearing estimates and provide visual map-matched drift correction.
- **Set an emoji alongside your username.** Optional, unset by default, and deliberately not built. Usernames identify members in chat and approval cards; an emoji would be a second, loosely-authenticated identity field hanging off a name that is already shown everywhere. If it is added, two decisions come first — whether it travels in the same signed payload as the name, and whether it is restricted to a small fixed set the way chat bubble colours are, so two phones cannot render the same member differently.
